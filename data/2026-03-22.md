### [16] [Lamba: A pretrained model for latency prediction over distributed databases](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_url?url=https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00778-026-00970-y&hl=zh-CN&sa=X&d=8283745523325010060&ei=mcW-aciyOJTP6rQPnpK_yAY&scisig=AFtJQixBYMPCVc4ClmichPBnlZIC&oi=scholaralrt&hist=Pxo5FIAAAAAJ:5415062280803925783:AFtJQiwBEs6WM0eozjMMVqiUyU2Z&html=&pos=0&folt=cit)
*Y Zhou,T Zeng,R Zhu,Y Li,J Lan,Z Wei,Y Fang…*

Main category: Ziniu Wu

TL;DR: Lamba is a pretrained, dual-stage model for zero-shot latency prediction in distributed databases, leveraging segment-specific and inter-segment features to achieve high accuracy and strong generalization with minimal model size.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing latency prediction methods either lack accuracy (traditional) or incur high overhead (ML-based), while current zero-shot approaches focus on centralized databases and fail to address the unique challenges of distributed databases—such as heterogeneous segments, data migration, and varying data distributions.

Method: Lamba uses a dual-stage architecture: a Segment Encoder captures per-node execution patterns using low-level, transferable features, and an Inter-Segment Interactor models interactions between segments. It is pretrained on execution statistics from 30 diverse datasets on Alibaba AnalyticDB and supports fine-tuning for adaptation.

Result: Lamba achieves superior prediction accuracy on unseen datasets with a compact model size, and demonstrates robust adaptability to new scenarios like data updates, scaling, workload shifts, and environment changes.

Conclusion: By aligning model design with the intrinsic characteristics of distributed databases, Lamba effectively captures latency-relevant meta-knowledge, enabling accurate zero-shot prediction and seamless generalization across diverse and dynamic environments.

Abstract: Latency prediction is crucial for databases, yet traditional methods lack accuracy while machine learning based approaches demand heavy overheads on data collection and model training. Existing zero-shot methods leverage pretraining to attain cross-database generality but primarily focus on centralized databases, overlooking the distributed databases widely used for processing large-scale data. Distributed databases with multiple segment nodes bring additional challenges for latency prediction, including the varying data distributions across segments, the inter-segment data migration during execution, heterogeneous environments and etc. To tackle these challenges, we introduce Lamba, a pretrained model tailored for latency prediction and aligning with the inherent characteristics of distributed databases. It employs low-level and transferable features and a dual-stage design mirroring query execution. Specifically, the Segment Encoder captures specific execution patterns on each segment node and the Inter-Segment Interactor models inter-segment interactions. In such way, Lamba naturally learns the intricate relations among these features, captures the meta-knowledge actually matters to latency prediction and generalizes across diverse data, queries, and environments. Pretrained on execution statistics from 30 diverse datasets deployed on Alibaba AnalyticDB, Lamba delivers superior accuracy on unseen datasets with a compact model size of only. It can be further improved with finetuning and adapts seamlessly to various, sometimes totally new, scenarios, including but not limited to data updating and scaling, workload drifts and environment changes.
